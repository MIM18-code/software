Writers Block Screenplay dictation for Final Draft that runs on your Mac

Click an empty paragraph in Final Draft, press Command-Option-D and talk through the scene: where you are, what happens, who says what. Press it again and the scene lands at your cursor as real Final Draft paragraphs, with the scene heading, action, character names, parentheticals and dialogue each in its own element. The speech recognition and the formatting both run on your Mac.

Download (166 MB) Version 0.3.1 beta. macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple Silicon. Free, MIT.

This is a beta. The formatting model gets most takes right, not every one. Read what it pasted the way you'd read any dictated draft. Every recording and transcript is saved, so a take that goes wrong can be fixed or run again.

What it does

  • Your words, labeled. The model decides which of your sentences is action and which is dialogue. It isn't asked to rewrite them. When a take drops or changes words, Writers Block pastes it anyway and tells you which words to check.
  • Talk the way you think. “Scratch that”, “no wait” and corrections partway through a sentence are handled. So is a heading you say in the middle of a sentence, like “she walks in, interior kitchen, night”.
  • Nothing is uploaded. Parakeet transcribes your recording and a writing model running in Ollama formats it, both on your Mac. On first launch, Writers Block walks you through installing Ollama and downloading the two models, about 6 GB in all.
  • Other writing apps too. In the Writers Block workspace you can record or paste a transcript, check the scene, then copy it, send it to any open app, or export Fountain, FDX or plain text.
  • Use the dictation app you have. If you already dictate with another app, paste its text into the workspace and Writers Block formats it the same way.

Questions

Do I need Final Draft?

No. Writers Block pastes into Final Draft if you have it, and its own workspace exports Fountain, FDX or plain text for any other writing app.

Is my voice sent anywhere?

No. The speech recognition and the formatting model both run on your Mac.

How much disk space does it need?

About 8 GB free. The two models it downloads on first launch are about 6 GB together.
